Educators Co-Building with AI


A Case Study in Instructional Coaching Transformation


August 2025 – March 2026


Overview


Most AI in education is built outside schools and then pushed into classrooms.This network took a different approach: educators co-built with AI inside their daily work.
This work took place in a multi-site public charter network in California, serving ~1,900 students across urban and suburban communities, including a high percentage of English learners and students qualifying for free and reduced-price lunch.


Across 4 school sites, 44 instructional coaches supported 189 teachers:
	•	1,781 classroom observations
	•	2,185 targeted action steps
	•	+0.44 improvement in observed instructional practice (~19%)


The result is a system that makes instructional change visible — not assumed.


The Challenge
Instructional coaching often generates activity without clear evidence of impact.
	•	Observations are conducted
	•	Feedback is given
	•	Action steps are assigned
But what happens next is often unclear.


As one instructional coach described: “Before this, I was guessing whether my feedback was landing. Now I can see it in the data before the next observation cycle.”
Without visibility, coaching becomes episodic — and impact is inferred rather than observed.


The Approach: Co-Building with AI


Rather than implementing a pre-built platform, this network co-developed a system alongside AI.
Educators defined:
	•	what effective instruction looks like
	•	how coaching feedback should be structured
	•	which instructional priorities matter most


AI supported the system by:
	•	organizing observation data in real time
	•	surfacing patterns across teachers and schools
	•	standardizing coaching language and structures
	•	reducing manual tracking and analysis


As one assistant principal noted:
“The system didn’t tell us what good instruction was. We defined that. The AI just made it visible across every classroom.”






The Coaching Model
The system is built around a continuous improvement cycle:
Observe → Assign → Revisit → Sustain
	•	Arc-aligned observations
Each observation reflects what should be happening at that point in the instructional calendar.
	•	Structured action steps
Feedback becomes a specific, named instructional move.
	•	Continuation tracking
The system captures whether instructional work is sustained across cycles.
	•	Shared visibility
Teachers, coaches, and leaders access the same data through role-specific views.


What the Data Shows
Instructional practice improved
Average observation scores increased from 2.29 in August to 2.73 in March:
	•	+0.44 points (~19% gain)
	•	Early-year average: 2.43
	•	Late-year average: 2.67


This reflects a sustained upward trajectory across the year.
Coaching is aligned to priority areas
	•	ELA (K–5): 400+ observations
	•	STEM (K–8): 400+
	•	Full Inclusion: 300+
	•	Small Group Instruction: highest performance (avg ~2.77)


Effort followed instructional need — not uniform distribution.
Coaching is sustained over time
	•	92% of teachers engaged in coaching cycles
	•	~14 action steps per teacher on average
	•	Up to 24 for the most-supported teachers


Rather than relying on one-time completion, the system reveals:
	•	which instructional priorities are revisited
	•	where coaching focus is sustained
	•	how practices evolve across multiple cycles


A shared instructional model emerged
Most frequently used coaching techniques include:
	•	Tracking, Not Watching (130+ uses)
	•	Instructional Excellence Amplification
	•	Culture
	•	Classroom Management


This reflects increasing alignment across classrooms and schools.


Instructional Area Insights


Performance varies across instructional roles:
	•	Accelerated Reader (AR) / Reading Intervention: ~2.98
	•	Small Group Instruction: ~2.77
	•	ELA and STEM: approaching proficiency
	•	Full Inclusion: ~2.41 (complex instructional context)
	•	Culture: ~2.26
	•	Campus Safety / Support (CST): ~1.68 (priority area)


As one school leader put it:


“We always knew some areas were weaker. This was the first time we could see exactly where — and how far.”


What Co-Building with AI Enabled
 Relevance
The system reflects real classrooms because educators defined it.


Consistency
AI ensures shared structures across sites.


Visibility
Patterns that were previously difficult to detect are now clear.


Adaptability
The system evolves as instructional priorities change.


Why This Matters
AI in education is often positioned as a replacement for educator judgment.
This model demonstrates a different use:
AI as infrastructure for instructional improvement
	•	not replacing educators
	•	not generating generic recommendations
	•	but making expertise visible and actionable at scale


The Result


Since August:
	•	1,781 observations conducted
	•	2,185 action steps assigned
	•	+0.44 improvement in instructional practice


No additional systems.
No increased administrative burden.
The system was co-built with educators — and powered by AI to sustain and scale their work.


Conclusion
Most coaching systems measure activity.
This model makes instructional change observable.
