# **Case Study**

## Turning Instructional Coaching Into a System

A K–8 public school network implemented NovaPath to bring structure and visibility to instructional coaching across four campuses.

In one school year, the system captured:

* **1,892 classroom observations**
* **244 teachers supported**
* **44 instructional coaches engaged**
* **Network-wide average score: 2.50 / 4.0**

This wasn’t a pilot.

It was live, in classrooms, with real instructional decisions being made every day.

---

## **From Activity to Insight**

Before implementation, coaching looked familiar:

* Observations happened regularly
* Feedback was delivered
* Follow-up varied by coach and campus

After implementation, something changed:

Every observation became part of a **connected system**.

Leaders could now see:

* Instructional performance across campuses
* Trends over time
* Differences between teams and sites

Not in theory — in real data.

---

## **What the System Revealed**

Across nearly 2,000 observations, clear patterns emerged:

### 1. Observation Volume Isn’t the Lever

Some campuses conducted more observations but did not outperform others.

Others conducted fewer observations and showed stronger results.

> More activity didn’t equal better outcomes.
> **How coaching was executed mattered more than how often it happened.**

---

### 2. Instructional Practice Improved Over Time

Every campus showed upward movement in instructional scores.

* Some sites improved by nearly **half a point over the year**
* Others reached or approached **proficiency benchmarks (3.0)**

> Instructional growth became visible — and measurable.

---

### 3. Consistency Drives Results

The strongest-performing campuses shared a common pattern:

* Stable score growth over time
* Less variability across classrooms
* More consistent instructional expectations

Other campuses showed more fluctuation.

> The difference wasn’t whether coaching existed —
> it was how consistently it showed up in practice.

---

### 4. Data Use Is Not Automatic

The system tracked whether core practices were actually happening:

* Structured data conversations between coaches and teachers
* Visibility of student performance data in classrooms

Adoption varied significantly across campuses.

> Having a system doesn’t guarantee use.
> Implementation determines impact.

---

## **What NovaPath Enables**

NovaPath didn’t introduce more work.

It made existing work visible, connected, and actionable.

With NovaPath, leaders could:

* See instructional performance across the entire system
* Identify where coaching was working — and where it wasn’t
* Track change over time, not just snapshots
* Move from isolated observations to system-level insight

---

Instead of asking:

And they could answer those questions with real data.
