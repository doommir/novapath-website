How to compare benefits alongside wages

By NovaPath · Published September 11, 2026

On this page
  1. Keep different measures separate
  2. Build a benefits comparison worksheet
  3. An illustrative calculation
  4. Avoid double counting
  5. What to provide for a scoped review

A benefits comparison should show what the employer contributes, who is eligible, and how the measure was calculated. A list of benefit names is not enough to establish equivalent value.

Keep different measures separate

Base pay, employer cost, and employee-perceived value answer different questions. An employer’s health contribution is a cost measure; its usefulness to an employee depends on coverage, eligibility, and personal circumstances. Avoid labeling all of these “total compensation” without defining the term.

Build a benefits comparison worksheet

ElementRecordClarify before comparing
Health coverageEmployer contribution and coverage tierEmployee-only versus family coverage, eligibility, and waiting periods
RetirementEmployer contribution formulaMatch conditions and whether the figure is a maximum or actual contribution
Paid leavePolicy and eligibilityWhether paid time is already included in the salary basis
Other benefitsDefined employer-paid itemsRecurring versus one-time benefits and participation assumptions

An illustrative calculation

Suppose annual base pay is $50,000, the employer contributes $6,000 toward the selected health coverage tier, and a defined employer retirement contribution is $1,500. The sum of those specified elements is $57,500.

Call it “base pay plus specified employer contributions.” It is not a comprehensive employer-cost figure unless all intended components are included. These fictional amounts do not represent NovaPath clients or market data.

Avoid double counting

If annual salary already pays an employee during leave, adding the salary value of those same days to annual cash pay can overstate the total. Define whether the report is comparing paid time, incremental employer expense, or a broader benefits package.

Use the same participation and eligibility assumptions for both sides of a comparison. Label unavailable information as unknown rather than treating it as no benefit.

What to provide for a scoped review

Prepare benefit summaries, employer contribution schedules, eligibility rules, and the coverage tiers that matter to the comparison. Agree with NovaPath on which elements are included and how missing information will be handled before analysis begins.
