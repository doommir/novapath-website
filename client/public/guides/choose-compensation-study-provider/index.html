How to choose a compensation study provider

By NovaPath · Published September 11, 2026

Choose a compensation study provider by examining how it will match your jobs, document its evidence, and help you use the findings. Compare written scope and deliverables alongside fee and turnaround.

Use the same questions for every provider

CompareAsk forWhy it matters
Job matchingThe process for comparing duties, qualifications, and responsibility.Similar titles can describe different work.
Market selectionThe rationale for geography and comparison employers.The selected market shapes the results.
EvidenceSource names, dates, coverage, and limitations.Your team should be able to trace the findings.
Pay and benefitsWhat is included and how schedules and units are normalized.Hourly pay, annual salary, and employer benefits are different measures.
DeliverablesAn outline of tables, narrative, recommendations, and supporting documentation.A data export and a decision-ready report serve different needs.
TimingIntake requirements, draft date, and final review schedule.Fast drafting does not eliminate preparation and review.
Fee and changesA scoped quote and the process for additional roles or revisions.Comparable quotes need comparable work.

Head Start, school, and nonprofit considerations

Describe your actual positions before selecting benchmarks. Include classroom versus administrative responsibilities, required credentials, supervisory duties, and annual work schedules. Ask the provider to explain when a broad occupation category is a weak match.

If your organization needs a report for a specific review or approval process, provide those requirements at scoping. Ask the provider to identify what the engagement covers and what requires separate review.

A short request you can send

We need a wage and compensation study covering [roles] in [locations] for [decision or purpose]. Please describe your job-matching approach, comparison sources, benefits coverage, deliverables, data requirements, draft timing, revision process, and scoped fee.
